Earlier this year I took over the website for a Chicago construction company and found that its contact form had stopped working. Nothing on the site looked wrong. A visitor could fill in the form, click Send, and see a thank-you message, but the form's email never reached anyone.

The only way to know your form works is to test it, and the test takes about five minutes.

Why contact forms stop working

  • Email providers don't trust the form's messages. Providers like Gmail check whether an email can prove it came from the sender's domain. Google's sender guidelines require every sender to set up SPF or DKIM, two settings that provide that proof. Many WordPress sites send form email straight from the web server without either one, and those messages are likely to end up in spam or not arrive at all. A plugin (an add-on) that sends through an email service is the usual fix.

  • The email goes to an address nobody reads. The form was set up years ago to send to an employee who has since left, or to the web vendor who built the site.

  • A paid add-on expired. On WordPress, forms usually run on plugins. When a plugin's license lapses, it stops getting updates and can break after the next WordPress update.

  • Spam protection blocks customers. A CAPTCHA (the "prove you're human" check) that is set too strict, or that breaks after an update, can stop legitimate visitors from sending.

How to test your contact form

  1. From your phone, fill in the form the way a customer would. Use a personal email address and write "Test, please ignore" in the message.

  2. Ask whoever is supposed to get website inquiries whether it arrived, and have them check their spam folder too.

  3. If it didn't arrive within a few minutes, ask your web vendor which address the form sends to and what service sends it. Ask them to write it down so the next person can find it.

  4. If it did arrive, look at which address received it. A shared address like info@ that more than one person reads is safer than one person's inbox.

  5. Put the same test on someone's calendar for the first Monday of each month.

Keep a copy of every submission

Ask your web vendor whether your form saves submissions anywhere besides email. Many form plugins can keep a copy of each entry on the site. On the site I rebuilt for that construction company, every submission is saved to a database before the notification email goes out. If an email fails, the inquiry is still saved, and the record shows that the email didn't send.
